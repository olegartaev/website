Freshwater fish evolution across Eurasia

How did freshwater fish diversity come to be the way it is?

I study how river history, geographic isolation and renewed contact shape lineages, phenotypes and species boundaries across freshwater landscapes.

Field ichthyology Phylogeography Integrative taxonomy Morphology Spatial analysis
Research

Freshwater landscapes as evolutionary systems.

The central aim is not simply to catalogue diversity, but to understand how geography and history generated it — and why different lines of evidence sometimes tell different stories.

01

Where are the real species boundaries?

Integrative taxonomy combines specimens, morphology and molecular evidence to test boundaries in geographically fragmented freshwater lineages.

02

How has drainage history shaped lineages?

Phylogeographic structure is interpreted in the context of isolation, river rearrangements and changing connections among basins.

03

Why can phenotype and ancestry disagree?

Contrasting morphological and mitochondrial patterns provide natural systems for testing alternative historical explanations rather than assuming a single narrative.

Flagship comparative system

Phoxinus across Eurasia

Broad geographic sampling makes Eurasian minnows a useful system for connecting local species-level questions with larger patterns of freshwater diversification, isolation and biogeographic history.

Field systems

The research starts in rivers.

Field collections create the geographic and biological framework for the work: populations are sampled as parts of real river systems, with specimens, phenotype and locality context kept connected from the beginning.

Field sampling River habitat In the field Freshwater landscape
From system to evidence

Built from the biological system outward.

The value of the workflow is the connection between field design, specimens, phenotype, molecular evidence and geography — not any one method in isolation.

01 · SYSTEM

Sampling design

River basins, populations and comparison groups are chosen around the biological question.

02 · MATERIAL

Specimens & phenotype

Voucher material and high-resolution imagery preserve taxonomic and phenotypic information.

03 · EVIDENCE

Morphology & molecules

Comparative morphology and current molecular datasets provide independent lines of evidence.

04 · CONTEXT

Geography & history

Spatial patterns are interpreted against drainage structure and biogeographic history.

This makes it possible to move from finding an unusual natural system to formulating and testing the next evolutionary question within the same research framework.

About

Oleg Artaev

Ichthyologist and Senior Research Scientist

Papanin Institute for Biology of Inland Waters, Russian Academy of Sciences. I work on freshwater fish diversity, taxonomy, phylogeography and biogeography, with an emphasis on field-based comparative systems across Eurasia.

I am particularly interested in collaborations where new molecular or analytical approaches can be connected to well-defined natural systems, strong sampling design and organismal evidence.
